OVERVIEW

The idea

what a limit means

A limit = the value a function settles into, and from when.

  • limt→∞​f(t)=L means: for large t, f(t) is as close to L as we want.
  • ε = the tolerance — how close counts as “equal.”
  • T = the threshold — how large t must be before that holds.
  • Order matters: T usually depends on ε.
Read it as Pick any ε. From some T onward, f(t) stays inside [L−ε, L+ε].
PART 1

Limits at infinity

the meaning

limt→∞​f(t)=L = f gets and stays within ε of L after some T.

Definition limt→∞​f(t)=L if: for every ε>0 there is a T such that ∣f(t)−L∣≤ε for all t≥T.

Asymptote ≠ “never touches”

  • 1+x1​→1 — clean horizontal asymptote.
  • xsinx​→0 — still tends to 0 even though it crosses the axis infinitely often.

Memorise these

  • constant: c→c
  • inverse power (k>0): t−k→0
  • exponential decay (c>1): c−t→0
🟢 Example 1 · basic limits
t→∞lim​(3+2−t−t1​)

Try it first. Stuck? Open the hint.

💡 Hint
Split into three separate limits, one per term.
✅ Solution
  1. constant 3→3
  2. 2−t→0 (decay, base 2>1)
  3. t1​→0 (inverse power)
  4. add: 3+0−0
✅ Limit =3
PART 2

Algebra of limits

combine the parts

Split a limit into parts, take each limit, recombine.

Rules (if f→a, g→b)
  • f±g→a±b
  • fg→ab
  • f/g→a/b  (needs b=0)
  • cf→ca;  fk→ak (needs a>0)
🟡 Example 2 · conjugate
t→∞lim​t1​2−4−t1​​​

Try it first. Stuck? Open the hint.

💡 Hint
Multiply top and bottom by the conjugate 2+4−1/t​.
✅ Solution
  1. direct sub gives 00​ — indeterminate
  2. times 2+4−1/t​2+4−1/t​​; numerator becomes 4−(4−t1​)=t1​
  3. so the expression =2+4−1/t​1​
  4. 4−1/t​→2, giving 2+21​
✅ Limit =41​
⚠️ Check with the lecturer The Tuesday slide ends at 21​, but this method gives 41​. Likely a slide typo — it’s in your Week 1 “questions to ask.”

Useful identity

  • A−B=A+BA2−B2​ — the trick behind conjugates.
NEVER A−B​=A​−B​. Roots do not split over subtraction.

Dominant term

  • Biggest-growing term wins. Factor it out: t3−3t2+2=t3(1−t1​+t32​).
  • Bracket →1, so f(t)/t3→1 — that’s what “dominant” means.
🟡 Example 3 · dominant term
t→∞lim​4t3+6t​t3−3t2+2​

Try it first. Stuck? Open the hint.

💡 Hint
Divide top and bottom by the highest power, t3.
✅ Solution
  1. divide top & bottom by t3
  2. top: 1−t3​+t32​→1
  3. bottom: 4+t5/26​→4
  4. quotient rule (bottom =0)
✅ Limit =41​

Sandwich theorem

  • Use it when a bounded wobble (like sint) blocks the algebra rules.
  • If f≤g≤h and f→a, h→a, then g→a.
  • e.g. −t1​≤tsint​≤t1​⇒tsint​→0.
🔴 Example 4 · Sandwich
t→∞lim​1+t4​2t2−t3/2cost​

Try it first. Stuck? Open the hint.

💡 Hint
Divide by t2, then bound the cost term with ∣cost∣≤1.
✅ Solution
  1. divide top & bottom by t2
  2. top: 2−t​cost​; since ​t​cost​​≤t​1​→0, top →2 (Sandwich)
  3. bottom: t41​+1​→1
  4. quotient rule
✅ Limit =2
PART 3

Infinite limits

unbounded growth

Some functions blow up. Same game, target is ±∞.

Definition limt→∞​f(t)=+∞ if: for every M there is a T with f(t)≥M for all t≥T. And limf=−∞⟺lim(−f)=+∞.

Grow to +∞

  • power (k>0): tk→+∞
  • exponential (c>1): ct→+∞
  • log: logt→+∞
Trap f→0 does not give f1​→+∞. You also need f>0. If f flips sign (like tsint​), f1​ has no limit.
🟡 Example 5 · to −∞
t→∞lim​t5+4​1−2t3​

Try it first. Stuck? Open the hint.

💡 Hint
Top behaves like −2t3, bottom like t5/2. Factor out those powers.
✅ Solution
  1. top =t3(t31​−2), bottom =t5/21+4/t5​
  2. ratio =t1/2⋅1+4/t5​t31​−2​
  3. fraction →−2, and t1/2→+∞
  4. (+∞)×(−2)
✅ Limit =−∞
🟡 Example 6 · to 0
t→∞lim​t3+t​t+sint​

Try it first. Stuck? Open the hint.

💡 Hint
Top grows like t, bottom like t3/2. Bound sint with the Sandwich theorem.
✅ Solution
  1. top =t(1+tsint​), bracket →1
  2. bottom =t3/21+1/t2​
  3. ratio =t1/21+1/t2​1+sint/t​: top →1, bottom →+∞
  4. finite ÷∞
✅ Limit =0
STOP · indeterminate
  • ∞−∞: with g=t2, f−g can be +∞, a constant, −∞, or no limit.
  • ∞∞​: t2t3​→∞, t3ct3​→c, t3t2​→0.
  • Never write ∞−∞=∞ or ∞∞​=∞. Simplify first.
PART 4

At a point & one-sided

limits near c

Same idea near a point c. Watch the two sides.

Definition limt→c​f(t)=L if: for every ε>0 there is δ>0 with ∣f(t)−L∣≤ε whenever 0<∣t−c∣≤δ. The 0< means f(c) itself is ignored.
  • Algebra + Sandwich work the same as at ∞.
  • Two-sided limit exists ⟺ left limit = right limit.
🟢 Example 7 · substitute
x→2lim​x−4x2​

Try it first. Stuck? Open the hint.

💡 Hint
Denominator is not 0 at x=2, so substitute directly.
✅ Solution
  1. at x=2, denominator =−2=0 ⇒ continuous
  2. substitute: −24​
✅ Limit =−2
🟡 Example 8 · factor & cancel
x→−2lim​x2−4x3+8​

Try it first. Stuck? Open the hint.

💡 Hint
It’s 00​: factor both, cancel (x+2).
✅ Solution
  1. 00​ ⇒ both share (x+2)
  2. x3+8=(x+2)(x2−2x+4), x2−4=(x+2)(x−2)
  3. cancel: x−2x2−2x+4​
  4. put x=−2: −412​
✅ Limit =−3
🟡 Example 9 · to +∞
x→1lim​(1−x)2x2−3x+3​

Try it first. Stuck? Open the hint.

💡 Hint
Numerator →1=0. What does (1−x)2 do near 1?
✅ Solution
  1. numerator at 1: 1−3+3=1>0
  2. denominator (1−x)2→0+
  3. positive ÷0+
✅ Limit =+∞

One-sided

  • limt→c−​ approaches from below, limt→c+​ from above.
  • Left = right (or one missing) ⇒ two-sided does not exist.
🔴 Example 10 · does not exist
x→1lim​1−x2x2−3x+3​

Try it first. Stuck? Open the hint.

💡 Hint
Check the sign of the denominator from the left and the right of 1.
✅ Solution
  1. numerator →1>0; denominator 1−x2=(1−x)(1+x), (1+x)→2>0
  2. x→1−: 1−x>0⇒ denom →0+⇒+∞
  3. x→1+: 1−x<0⇒ denom →0−⇒−∞
  4. left = right
✅ Two-sided limit does not exist (DNE)
STOP · indeterminate forms ∞−∞, 00​, ∞∞​, 0⋅∞, 1∞ — never plug straight in. Simplify (factor / conjugate / dominant term) first.

Which trick when

  • rational / roots at ∞ → dominant term (divide by highest power)
  • a− ⋅ ​ shape → conjugate
  • bounded wobble (sin,cos) → Sandwich

Definitions in one line

  • at ∞: ∀ε ∃T ∀t≥T, ∣f−L∣≤ε
  • +∞: ∀M ∃T ∀t≥T, f≥M
  • at c: ∀ε ∃δ ∀t, 0<∣t−c∣≤δ, ∣f−L∣≤ε
NEVER
  • A−B​=A​−B​
  • no ∞−∞=∞, no ∞∞​=∞
