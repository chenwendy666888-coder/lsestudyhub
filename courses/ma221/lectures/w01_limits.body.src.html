OVERVIEW

The idea

what a limit means

A limit = the value a function settles into, and from when.

  • $\lim_{t\to\infty} f(t)=L$ means: for large $t$, $f(t)$ is as close to $L$ as we want.
  • $\varepsilon$ = the tolerance — how close counts as “equal.”
  • $T$ = the threshold — how large $t$ must be before that holds.
  • Order matters: $T$ usually depends on $\varepsilon$.
Read it as Pick any $\varepsilon$. From some $T$ onward, $f(t)$ stays inside $[L-\varepsilon,\ L+\varepsilon]$.
PART 1

Limits at infinity

the meaning

$\lim_{t\to\infty} f(t)=L$ = $f$ gets and stays within $\varepsilon$ of $L$ after some $T$.

Definition $\lim_{t\to\infty} f(t)=L$ if: for every $\varepsilon>0$ there is a $T$ such that $|f(t)-L|\le\varepsilon$ for all $t\ge T$.

Asymptote ≠ “never touches”

  • $1+\tfrac1x \to 1$ — clean horizontal asymptote.
  • $\tfrac{\sin x}{x} \to 0$ — still tends to $0$ even though it crosses the axis infinitely often.

Memorise these

  • constant: $c \to c$
  • inverse power ($k>0$): $t^{-k} \to 0$
  • exponential decay ($c>1$): $c^{-t} \to 0$
🟢 Example 1 · basic limits
$$\lim_{t\to\infty}\Big(3+2^{-t}-\tfrac1t\Big)$$

Try it first. Stuck? Open the hint.

💡 Hint
Split into three separate limits, one per term.
✅ Solution
  1. constant $3 \to 3$
  2. $2^{-t} \to 0$ (decay, base $2>1$)
  3. $\tfrac1t \to 0$ (inverse power)
  4. add: $3+0-0$
✅ Limit $=3$
PART 2

Algebra of limits

combine the parts

Split a limit into parts, take each limit, recombine.

Rules (if $f\to a,\ g\to b$)
  • $f\pm g \to a\pm b$
  • $fg \to ab$
  • $f/g \to a/b$  (needs $b\ne0$)
  • $cf \to ca$;  $f^{k}\to a^{k}$ (needs $a>0$)
🟡 Example 2 · conjugate
$$\lim_{t\to\infty}\frac{2-\sqrt{4-\tfrac1t}}{\tfrac1t}$$

Try it first. Stuck? Open the hint.

💡 Hint
Multiply top and bottom by the conjugate $2+\sqrt{4-1/t}$.
✅ Solution
  1. direct sub gives $\tfrac00$ — indeterminate
  2. times $\dfrac{2+\sqrt{4-1/t}}{2+\sqrt{4-1/t}}$; numerator becomes $4-(4-\tfrac1t)=\tfrac1t$
  3. so the expression $=\dfrac{1}{\,2+\sqrt{4-1/t}\,}$
  4. $\sqrt{4-1/t}\to2$, giving $\dfrac1{2+2}$
✅ Limit $=\tfrac14$
⚠️ Check with the lecturer The Tuesday slide ends at $\tfrac12$, but this method gives $\tfrac14$. Likely a slide typo — it’s in your Week 1 “questions to ask.”

Useful identity

  • $A-B=\dfrac{A^2-B^2}{A+B}$ — the trick behind conjugates.
NEVER $\sqrt{A-B}\ne\sqrt A-\sqrt B$. Roots do not split over subtraction.

Dominant term

  • Biggest-growing term wins. Factor it out: $t^3-3t^2+2=t^3\big(1-\tfrac1t+\tfrac2{t^3}\big)$.
  • Bracket $\to1$, so $f(t)/t^3\to1$ — that’s what “dominant” means.
🟡 Example 3 · dominant term
$$\lim_{t\to\infty}\frac{t^3-3t^2+2}{4t^3+6\sqrt t}$$

Try it first. Stuck? Open the hint.

💡 Hint
Divide top and bottom by the highest power, $t^3$.
✅ Solution
  1. divide top & bottom by $t^3$
  2. top: $1-\tfrac3t+\tfrac2{t^3}\to1$
  3. bottom: $4+\tfrac6{t^{5/2}}\to4$
  4. quotient rule (bottom $\ne0$)
✅ Limit $=\tfrac14$

Sandwich theorem

  • Use it when a bounded wobble (like $\sin t$) blocks the algebra rules.
  • If $f\le g\le h$ and $f\to a,\ h\to a$, then $g\to a$.
  • e.g. $-\tfrac1t\le\tfrac{\sin t}{t}\le\tfrac1t \Rightarrow \tfrac{\sin t}{t}\to0$.
🔴 Example 4 · Sandwich
$$\lim_{t\to\infty}\frac{2t^2-t^{3/2}\cos t}{\sqrt{1+t^4}}$$

Try it first. Stuck? Open the hint.

💡 Hint
Divide by $t^2$, then bound the $\cos t$ term with $|\cos t|\le1$.
✅ Solution
  1. divide top & bottom by $t^2$
  2. top: $2-\tfrac{\cos t}{\sqrt t}$; since $\big|\tfrac{\cos t}{\sqrt t}\big|\le\tfrac1{\sqrt t}\to0$, top $\to2$ (Sandwich)
  3. bottom: $\sqrt{\tfrac1{t^4}+1}\to1$
  4. quotient rule
✅ Limit $=2$
PART 3

Infinite limits

unbounded growth

Some functions blow up. Same game, target is $\pm\infty$.

Definition $\lim_{t\to\infty} f(t)=+\infty$ if: for every $M$ there is a $T$ with $f(t)\ge M$ for all $t\ge T$. And $\lim f=-\infty \iff \lim (-f)=+\infty$.

Grow to $+\infty$

  • power ($k>0$): $t^k\to+\infty$
  • exponential ($c>1$): $c^t\to+\infty$
  • log: $\log t\to+\infty$
Trap $f\to0$ does not give $\tfrac1f\to+\infty$. You also need $f>0$. If $f$ flips sign (like $\tfrac{\sin t}{t}$), $\tfrac1f$ has no limit.
🟡 Example 5 · to $-\infty$
$$\lim_{t\to\infty}\frac{1-2t^3}{\sqrt{t^5+4}}$$

Try it first. Stuck? Open the hint.

💡 Hint
Top behaves like $-2t^3$, bottom like $t^{5/2}$. Factor out those powers.
✅ Solution
  1. top $=t^3(\tfrac1{t^3}-2)$, bottom $=t^{5/2}\sqrt{1+4/t^5}$
  2. ratio $=t^{1/2}\cdot\dfrac{\tfrac1{t^3}-2}{\sqrt{1+4/t^5}}$
  3. fraction $\to-2$, and $t^{1/2}\to+\infty$
  4. $(+\infty)\times(-2)$
✅ Limit $=-\infty$
🟡 Example 6 · to $0$
$$\lim_{t\to\infty}\frac{t+\sin t}{\sqrt{t^3+t}}$$

Try it first. Stuck? Open the hint.

💡 Hint
Top grows like $t$, bottom like $t^{3/2}$. Bound $\sin t$ with the Sandwich theorem.
✅ Solution
  1. top $=t(1+\tfrac{\sin t}{t})$, bracket $\to1$
  2. bottom $=t^{3/2}\sqrt{1+1/t^2}$
  3. ratio $=\dfrac{1+\sin t/t}{t^{1/2}\sqrt{1+1/t^2}}$: top $\to1$, bottom $\to+\infty$
  4. finite $\div\infty$
✅ Limit $=0$
STOP · indeterminate
  • $\infty-\infty$: with $g=t^2$, $f-g$ can be $+\infty$, a constant, $-\infty$, or no limit.
  • $\tfrac{\infty}{\infty}$: $\tfrac{t^3}{t^2}\to\infty$, $\tfrac{ct^3}{t^3}\to c$, $\tfrac{t^2}{t^3}\to0$.
  • Never write $\infty-\infty=\infty$ or $\tfrac{\infty}{\infty}=\infty$. Simplify first.
PART 4

At a point & one-sided

limits near c

Same idea near a point $c$. Watch the two sides.

Definition $\lim_{t\to c} f(t)=L$ if: for every $\varepsilon>0$ there is $\delta>0$ with $|f(t)-L|\le\varepsilon$ whenever $0<|t-c|\le\delta$. The $0<$ means $f(c)$ itself is ignored.
  • Algebra + Sandwich work the same as at $\infty$.
  • Two-sided limit exists $\iff$ left limit $=$ right limit.
🟢 Example 7 · substitute
$$\lim_{x\to2}\frac{x^2}{x-4}$$

Try it first. Stuck? Open the hint.

💡 Hint
Denominator is not $0$ at $x=2$, so substitute directly.
✅ Solution
  1. at $x=2$, denominator $=-2\ne0$ ⇒ continuous
  2. substitute: $\dfrac{4}{-2}$
✅ Limit $=-2$
🟡 Example 8 · factor & cancel
$$\lim_{x\to-2}\frac{x^3+8}{x^2-4}$$

Try it first. Stuck? Open the hint.

💡 Hint
It’s $\tfrac00$: factor both, cancel $(x+2)$.
✅ Solution
  1. $\tfrac00$ ⇒ both share $(x+2)$
  2. $x^3+8=(x+2)(x^2-2x+4)$, $x^2-4=(x+2)(x-2)$
  3. cancel: $\dfrac{x^2-2x+4}{x-2}$
  4. put $x=-2$: $\dfrac{12}{-4}$
✅ Limit $=-3$
🟡 Example 9 · to $+\infty$
$$\lim_{x\to1}\frac{x^2-3x+3}{(1-x)^2}$$

Try it first. Stuck? Open the hint.

💡 Hint
Numerator $\to1\ne0$. What does $(1-x)^2$ do near $1$?
✅ Solution
  1. numerator at $1$: $1-3+3=1>0$
  2. denominator $(1-x)^2\to0^+$
  3. positive $\div\,0^+$
✅ Limit $=+\infty$

One-sided

  • $\lim_{t\to c^-}$ approaches from below, $\lim_{t\to c^+}$ from above.
  • Left $\ne$ right (or one missing) $\Rightarrow$ two-sided does not exist.
🔴 Example 10 · does not exist
$$\lim_{x\to1}\frac{x^2-3x+3}{1-x^2}$$

Try it first. Stuck? Open the hint.

💡 Hint
Check the sign of the denominator from the left and the right of $1$.
✅ Solution
  1. numerator $\to1>0$; denominator $1-x^2=(1-x)(1+x)$, $(1+x)\to2>0$
  2. $x\to1^-$: $1-x>0 \Rightarrow$ denom $\to0^+ \Rightarrow +\infty$
  3. $x\to1^+$: $1-x<0 \Rightarrow$ denom $\to0^- \Rightarrow -\infty$
  4. left $\ne$ right
✅ Two-sided limit does not exist (DNE)
STOP · indeterminate forms $\infty-\infty,\ \tfrac00,\ \tfrac{\infty}{\infty},\ 0\cdot\infty,\ 1^{\infty}$ — never plug straight in. Simplify (factor / conjugate / dominant term) first.

One-page recap

Which trick when

  • rational / roots at $\infty$ → dominant term (divide by highest power)
  • $a-\sqrt{\ \cdot\ }$ shape → conjugate
  • bounded wobble ($\sin,\cos$) → Sandwich
  • $\tfrac00$ at a point → factor & cancel
  • $\tfrac{\text{nonzero}}{0}$ → check left/right sign for $\pm\infty$ or DNE

Definitions in one line

  • at $\infty$: $\forall\varepsilon\ \exists T\ \forall t\ge T,\ |f-L|\le\varepsilon$
  • $+\infty$: $\forall M\ \exists T\ \forall t\ge T,\ f\ge M$
  • at $c$: $\forall\varepsilon\ \exists\delta\ \forall t,\ 0<|t-c|\le\delta,\ |f-L|\le\varepsilon$
NEVER
  • $\sqrt{A-B}\ne\sqrt A-\sqrt B$
  • no $\infty-\infty=\infty$, no $\tfrac{\infty}{\infty}=\infty$
